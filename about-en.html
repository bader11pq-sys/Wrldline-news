Our promise to readers

We explain public affairs, the economy, and global events for readers of Arabic. We distinguish reporting from interpretation and opinion. We credit the original publisher when aggregating a story rather than reproducing it in full.

Our event files bring dated sources and developments together. Unlike a daily report, an event file shows what changed and what still needs evidence.

Editorial independence

A sponsor cannot buy news coverage or change the outcome of an editorial review. Advertising and sponsorship are separate from reporting. Sponsored material is labelled and the sponsor is identified. A material conflict of interest is disclosed or the work is reassigned.

Attribution and correction rules apply to sponsored material too. Partnership requests go through the contact form, not the story selection process.

How we distinguish facts from claims

We look for the original document or statement and check its time, place, and context. Statements are attributed. An official release can show what an organization said without proving everything it said. We seek independent corroboration of material claims where possible and explain what we could not verify.

Unverified
Available evidence does not yet establish the claim. The label does not mean the claim is false.
Multiple sources
More than one source carried the information. Multiple copies of the same wire report do not add independent corroboration.
Disputed
Accounts or evidence conflict. We explain where they disagree and what can be checked.
Confirmed
Reserved for evidence-backed editorial confirmation, not a link count or an automated score.

Breaking is a display priority, not a verification grade. Analysis explains significance, while opinion reflects the writer's judgment. Sensitive allegations are not published automatically.

Source policy

Fitness for a claim depends on proximity, method, timing, and independence. Inclusion in the source directory is not an endorsement of everything a publisher produces.

Primary record or official statement
Link to the original and identify its author, date, and what it establishes.
Newsroom or wire report
Credit the original reporting operation and distinguish republication from independent reporting.
Research or rights report
Name the organization, date, method, and limitations. Distinguish observations from recommendations.
Witness or online material
Look for the original and its context. Check details against other evidence and do not expose a person's identity if it could put them at risk.

Translation and automation

Automation can assist with classification, summaries, or translation drafts. It is not a source and does not verify claims. When a reviewed edition is unavailable, consult the original source. We distinguish publication time from receipt and update times.

Attribution and rights

We name the source and link to it when available. Rights in original content remain with the rights holders. Display here does not grant permission to reuse it. Submit copyright questions through the contact form.

To report a published error or request a reply, see corrections and right of reply. Do not send sensitive documents through the public contact form.
